## §1. The Log-Periodic Anomaly

### 1.1 What is Log-Periodicity?

In standard RG flows, physical quantities scale as power laws:

$$ O(\mu) \propto \mu^\Delta $$

where Δ is the scaling dimension. This is "continuous scale invariance" — dilations by any factor produce a simple rescaling.

**Discrete scale invariance** (DSI) is a weaker form: the system is invariant only under dilations by specific discrete factors λ^n. Observable quantities then oscillate logarithmically:

$$ O(\mu) = \mu^\Delta \cdot F(\ln\mu / \ln\lambda) $$

where F(x + 1) = F(x) is a periodic function. This produces **log-periodic oscillations** — a signature that something non-trivial is happening in the RG flow.

### 1.2 Why It Matters for Pillar I

Pillar I states: scale separation ≡ energy-level separation. The harmonic oscillator has equally spaced energy levels: ΔE = ħω. Under an RG transformation that halves the momentum cutoff (scale factor b = 2), the effective theory changes discretely — exactly the scale analog of climbing the HO's energy ladder one rung at a time.

If the RG β-function admits a limit cycle (rather than flowing to a fixed point), the system exhibits discrete scale invariance with a characteristic scaling factor λ = e^(2π/|β*|) where β* is the β-function at the cycle. This is the RG analog of the harmonic oscillator's discrete spectrum — scale as energy level.

**RQ3 asks:** Is this a generic feature of RG flows with limit cycles, and where can we find experimental evidence?

## §2. The Efimov Effect: The Canonical Example

### 2.1 Three-Body Physics

The Efimov effect (1970) is the best-established example of discrete scale invariance in quantum physics. In a system of three identical bosons with short-range interactions tuned to the unitary limit (infinite scattering length), an infinite tower of bound states appears with energies:

$$ E_n = E_0 \cdot (e^{\pi/s_0})^{-2n} \approx E_0 \cdot (22.7)^{-2n} $$

where s_0 ≈ 1.00624 is a universal number determined by the three-body problem. The binding energies form a geometric series with ratio ~1/515.

**Experimental confirmation:**
- Cold atom experiments (Innsbruck 2006, MIT 2006, Heidelberg 2014) observed Efimov trimers in Cs, Li, and K atoms
- The scaling factor λ = e^(π/s_0) ≈ 22.7 has been measured to ~10% precision
- Efimov states in ^4He trimers (Kunitski et al. 2015) — the helium dimer's anomalously large size is an Efimov precursor

### 2.2 The RG Origin

The Efimov effect arises from an RG limit cycle in the three-body sector. In momentum space, the three-body bound-state equation at the unitary limit exhibits an attractive 1/r² potential in the hyperradial coordinate:

$$ \left[-\frac{d^2}{dR^2} + \frac{s_0^2 + 1/4}{R^2}\right] \psi(R) = E\psi(R) $$

The 1/R² potential is scale-invariant (it has no intrinsic length scale) but leads to the "quantum anomaly" of dimensional transmutation: a scale is generated by the boundary condition at short distances. The RG flow in the three-body coupling constant traces a limit cycle, producing the geometric spectrum.

**Key insight for Pillar I:** The Efimov spectrum E_n ∝ λ^(−2n) is structurally identical to the harmonic oscillator spectrum E_n ∝ n, but with exponential rather than linear spacing. Both arise from discrete structure in the spectrum of a self-adjoint operator — the RG β-function in one case, the Hamiltonian in the other.

## §3. Candidate Systems for Log-Periodic Signatures

### 3.1 Cold Atomic Gases (Confirmed)

**System:** Ultracold bosons near a Feshbach resonance
**Signature:** Efimov trimers, tetramers, and loss-rate oscillations
**Status:** **Confirmed** — multiple experimental groups (2006–2020)
**Scaling factor:** λ ≈ 22.7

Beyond Efimov trimers, four-body (tetramer) states connected to Efimov trimers have been observed (Fer+2009, Poll+2009). The four-body parameter introduces a second limit cycle, with a universal ratio between three- and four-body parameters.

### 3.2 Quantum Critical Metals (Candidate)

**System:** Strongly correlated electron systems near a quantum critical point (QCP)
**Signature:** Log-periodic oscillations in:
- Resistivity ρ(T) at low temperatures
- Specific heat C/T
- Magnetic susceptibility

**Theoretical basis:** Certain non-Fermi liquid fixed points in 2+1 dimensions can exhibit limit-cycle behavior when the number of fermion flavors N_f is near the critical value for chiral symmetry breaking. The RG flow of the four-Fermi coupling g traces a limit cycle in the complex plane when the β-function has a non-zero imaginary part at the fixed point.

**Experimental status:** Hints of log-periodic oscillations have been reported in:
- Heavy fermion compounds (CeCu_6−xAu_x, YbRh₂Si₂) near their antiferromagnetic QCPs
- High-T_c cuprates — oscillations in the pseudogap temperature T*(p) as a function of doping p
- But: no unambiguous confirmation; these systems are noisy and log-periodic fitting has low statistical power

### 3.3 Turbulence and Fractals (Partial Evidence)

**System:** Turbulent flows, fracture patterns, financial time series
**Signature:** Log-periodic oscillations in structure functions, avalanche size distributions
**Status:** Controversial — log-periodic fits are flexible and prone to overfitting

**Theoretical basis:** The RG for turbulence (Kolmogorov 1941, perturbative corrections) does not naturally produce limit cycles. However, discrete scale invariance appears in systems with a built-in "preferred scaling ratio," such as:
- Diffusion-limited aggregation (fractal growth with discrete branching)
- Rock fracture and earthquake precursors (Sornette et al.)
- Stock market crashes (Sornette-Johansen log-periodic bubble model)

The RG-harmonic interpretation: in each case, the "energy levels" are discrete events (avalanches, bubbles, fractures) separated by scale factors that follow from the underlying instability mechanism. The universality of log-periodicity across these systems suggests a common RG structure — but the connection to QFT β-functions is tenuous.

### 3.4 Conformal Field Theories with Complex Dimensions (Theoretical)

**System:** CFTs with complex scaling dimensions (non-unitary theories)
**Signature:** Log-periodic correlators: ⟨O(x)O(0)⟩ ∝ |x|^(−2Δ) · cos(Δ_I ln|x| + φ)

**Theoretical basis:** In non-unitary CFTs, operators can acquire complex scaling dimensions Δ = Δ_R + iΔ_I. This occurs in:
- Logarithmic CFTs (e.g., c = −2 ghost system)
- Replica limits of disordered systems
- CFTs with c > 1 and non-compact target spaces

The log-periodic structure appears in correlation functions and is a direct consequence of complex conformal dimensions. The "log-period" in real space maps to a limit cycle in the space of scaling operators.

### 3.5 Cosmological Correlators (Speculative)

**System:** Primordial density perturbations (CMB, large-scale structure)
**Signature:** Log-periodic oscillations in the primordial power spectrum P(k)

**Theoretical basis:** Models with discrete scale invariance in the early universe (e.g., "boundary inflation" with a periodic potential, or ekpyrotic/cyclic models with a discrete bounce structure) can produce oscillations in ln(k). The Planck 2018 data shows a slight (~2σ) preference for log-periodic features at k ~ 0.05 Mpc⁻¹, but this is widely attributed to a look-elsewhere effect.

**RG-harmonic connection:** If the early-universe dynamics involved an RG limit cycle (e.g., from a periodic inflaton potential with a cosine term — structurally analogous to the transmon!), then primordial oscillations would be the cosmological analog of Efimov states. The transmon's cosine potential (RQ1) and the inflaton's cosine potential share the same mathematical structure; both can produce discrete scale spectra.

## §4. The p-Adic Connection

### 4.1 Discrete Scale Invariance as Ultrametricity

Discrete scale invariance with scaling factor λ is mathematically equivalent to an **ultrametric structure** on the space of scales. Under dilations by λ^n, scales form a tree-like hierarchy:

```
Scale:    λ⁰   λ¹   λ²   λ³   λ⁴   ...
           \   /    \   /    \   /
            \ /      \ /      \ /
           parent   parent   parent
```

This is a Bruhat-Tits tree — the geometric realization of p-adic space T_p for p = λ. The p-adic absolute value |x|_p = p^(−ord_p(x)) is naturally discrete, and functions on T_p are naturally log-periodic.

**The connection to RQ7 (P-adic HO):** If the harmonic oscillator has a p-adic analog, its spectrum should be log-periodic on the Bruhat-Tits tree. The Efimov spectrum E_n ∝ λ^(−2n) is precisely the spectrum of a p-adic harmonic oscillator on T_λ with frequency determined by s_0.

### 4.2 Adelic Unification

The adelic perspective: Efimov physics is the p-adic place of a more general phenomenon. The Archimedean place (continuous scale invariance, power laws) and the ultrametric place (discrete scale invariance, log-periodic oscillations) are unified in the adelic RG. Physical systems can exhibit both — continuous scaling in some observables, discrete scaling in others — depending on which "place" of the adelic RG is dominant.

### 5.2 Statistical Power Considerations

Log-periodic fitting is notorious for false positives. To claim detection at >5σ:
1. **Pre-register the analysis:** specify the fitting function, search range for λ, and look-elsewhere correction BEFORE analyzing the data
2. **Multiple harmonics:** a true limit cycle produces multiple log-periods, not just one oscillation
3. **Universality:** the same λ must appear across multiple observables in the same system
4. **p-value calibration:** test against surrogate data with the same power spectrum but no log-periodic structure
